What earns points?

Full named-sponsor credit

1 point: symbolic, recognition and advocacy measures.
2 points: routine property / administrative approvals.
5 points: fiscal measures, local policy and ordinances.

A symbolic resolution with seven named sponsors earns 1 point for each sponsor. A substantive item earns each named sponsor 5. Each distinct measure counts once per member, even if considered at several meetings. Earlier-year measures considered in 2026 are included and labeled in the audit.

Additional explicit cosponsorships are displayed separately with zero score credit. Moving, seconding or voting for an item does not establish sponsorship. No first-listed name is assumed to be the lead author.

Points and index are different

Points = symbolic sponsorships + 2 × administrative sponsorships + 5 × substantive sponsorships.

Index = 100 × points ÷ cohort mean points. The current mean is points. An index of 100 means cohort-average observed activity; it is not 100% or a good-performance threshold.

One primary topic and up to two secondary tags describe each measure. Tags do not alter the weight. Topic totals allocate points to the primary topic once.

Leadership and coverage

Leadership titles are shown as listed in the official council directory at review. They are context, not a verified history of every leadership appointment. No leadership penalty or expected-performance benchmark is applied. A stronger leadership benchmark requires a justified model and more observations.

The administrative approval remains in the measure inventory even where no named sponsor is identified; no sponsor is invented. Incomplete records, unobserved committee work and missing substantive amendments limit interpretation. There is no passage or enactment multiplier. A passed procedural motion is not proof the policy became law.

How this differs from CEL

The Center for Effective Lawmaking evaluates primary-sponsored bills, their advancement and substantive importance. Ordinary cosponsorship does not independently earn standard LES points. Its state methodology, Appendix, “Selecting among Multiple Primary Sponsors” normally identifies one introducing sponsor rather than crediting all sponsors and cosponsors. This municipal index instead credits every explicitly named joint sponsor because these extracts do not reliably establish a lead author. Its 1–2–5 weights are RIEP assumptions, not CEL categories or weights.

Future statewide comparisons

This is a within-Cranston comparison. A statewide municipal ranking would need common coverage periods, comparable sponsorship attribution and consistent coding across councils. State House and Senate effectiveness scores use different institutions and methods and should remain separate.
